# One

The quarry had been dry since the spring. Elena walked the rim of it in the early
part of the afternoon, counting the places where the rock had given way over the
winter and noting each of them in the small book she carried. The work was slow
and it suited her. She had done it every March for eleven years and the rhythm of
it had long since stopped requiring any thought at all.

Marcus arrived later than he had promised. He had driven up from the coast that
morning and he had not taken off his coat, and he stood at the gate for a while
before he came down to where she was working.

"You could have told me," he said.

She closed the book and put it in her pocket. Below them the floor of the quarry
lay pale and even, and the light went out of the afternoon slowly, the way it
always did that far north.

"I could have," she said. "You would have come anyway."

He did not answer that. They walked the last stretch of the rim together and
neither of them said very much, and when they reached the far gate the wind had
picked up off the water and it was cold enough that they both turned back.
