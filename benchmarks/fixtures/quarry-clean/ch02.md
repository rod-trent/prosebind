# Two

The village hall smelled of scorched milk and wet coats. Ruth had set out the cups
before anyone arrived, which was what she always did, and she stood near the urn
with her hands folded and watched the door.

Elena came in last. She took a cup she did not drink from and stood by the window,
watching the rain come across the fields in long grey sheets that reached the road
and then the yard and then the glass in front of her.

"He would have hated this," Ruth said.

"He would have hated all of it," Elena said. "The tea especially."

Ruth laughed, which surprised them both, and then she put her hand on the table as
if to steady herself and did not laugh again. The rain went on. Somebody at the
other end of the hall had started talking about the road, and the drainage, and
whether the council had any intention of doing anything about either.
