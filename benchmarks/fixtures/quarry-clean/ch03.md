# Three

Elena drove back to the quarry alone on the Tuesday. She parked at the gate and
walked the last stretch, and the wind came hard off the water and the gulls turned
above her in the failing light of an afternoon that was already going.

The water had come back. It had come back over the winter, quietly, the way it
always came, and it stood now across the whole floor of the quarry and reached
almost to the lower ledge where the machinery had been. She had known it would.
She had written it in the small book every year and nobody had ever read it.

Marcus had been right about the letter and she had never told him so. That was the
part she kept returning to, standing there with the cold coming up off the water
and the light going, and it seemed to her that there had been a great many
afternoons when she could have said it and had not.

She stayed until the light was gone. Then she walked back to the gate, and drove
home the long way, past the coast road and the turning she had not taken in years.
